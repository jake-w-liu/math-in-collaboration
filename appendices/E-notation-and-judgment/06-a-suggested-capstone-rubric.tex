\section{A suggested capstone rubric}\label{appE:sec:rubric}
The table below suggests one way to assess the independent capstone of Section~\ref{ch18:sec:independent}. The row ``Understanding'' assesses the short oral discussion that ends the investigation; the other rows follow that section's list of what the write-up should contain.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{1.15in}p{0.5in}X@{}}\toprule
Quality & Weight & What a strong project shows\\\midrule
Claim and scope & 20\% & The exact objects, hypotheses and conclusion of each result. If the question changed during the project, both versions are stated and the difference is explained. Each claim is marked with its evidence: proved, cited from another source, checked by computer for finitely many cases, or not established.\\
Argument & 35\% & A complete proof or a counterexample, with a map of the background the argument needs. The delicate steps are written out in full, and each step names the results it depends on.\\
Understanding & 20\% & In the oral discussion, the student explains a delicate step without help and, when an assumption or an example is changed, correctly predicts what still holds and what fails.\\
Reproducibility & 15\% & Computer checks where relevant, with the program, the range of cases covered and the software used, so that anyone can repeat them. Otherwise, an explanation of how a reader can check the claims without a computer, for example by working small cases by hand.\\
Context & 10\% & Accurate credit to every source used; a modest statement of what is new compared with the published literature and what is new only to the student; an open account of how an AI assistant was used and of what remains unsettled.\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Three faults cannot be outweighed by good presentation: a statement changed without saying so, a missing step on which the argument depends, and an unsupported claim of novelty. A project with any of them must be corrected before it can pass. The rubric rewards careful work on a small investigation of the student's own; nobody is expected to solve a famous problem.
