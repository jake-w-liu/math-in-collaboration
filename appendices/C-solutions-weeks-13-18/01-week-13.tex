\section{Week 13}
\sol{13.1}{The probabilities add up to $1$: $\tfrac12+\tfrac13+\tfrac16=\tfrac36+\tfrac26+\tfrac16=1$. By the formula $\E X=\sum_i x_i\Prob(X=x_i)$ of Section~\ref{ch13:sec:expectation},
\[
\E X=0\cdot\tfrac12+2\cdot\tfrac13+5\cdot\tfrac16=\tfrac46+\tfrac56=\tfrac96=\tfrac32.
\]
Both $2$ and $5$ are at least $3/2$, as Proposition~\ref{ch13:prop:above-average} guarantees for some value of positive probability.}
\sol{13.2}{Write a labeling by listing the labels of the three vertices in a fixed order; there are $2^3=8$ labelings. In $000$ and $111$ all three vertices are on the same side, and the cut has size $0$. In each of the other six, two vertices share a label and the third has the other label; the two edges at the third vertex cross, and the edge joining the other two does not, so the cut has size $2$. The possible cut sizes are therefore $0$ and $2$, and the average over all eight labelings is
\[
\frac{2\cdot0+6\cdot2}{8}=\frac{12}{8}=\frac32,
\]
half of the three edges, as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cut}. The average is not a possible cut size, but the labelings with cut size $2$ do at least as well as the average.}
\sol{13.3}{There are $m=4$ edges, so at the start $C=0$, $U=4$, and $F=2$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Vertex $1$ has no placed neighbors, so $\ell=r=0$, and the tie puts it on the left. No edge is settled yet, so $F=2$.
\item Vertex $2$ has $1$ on the left ($3$ is not yet placed), so $\ell=1$, $r=0$; place $2$ on the right. Edge $12$ crosses, so $C=1$, $U=3$, and $F=1+3/2=5/2$.
\item Vertex $3$ has $2$ on the right ($4$ is not yet placed), so $\ell=0$, $r=1$; place $3$ on the left. Edge $23$ crosses, so $C=2$, $U=2$, and $F=3$.
\item Vertex $4$ has $3$ and $1$ on the left, so $\ell=2$, $r=0$; place $4$ on the right. Edges $34$ and $41$ cross, so $C=4$, $U=0$, and $F=4$.
\end{itemize}
Each increase of $F$ equals $|\ell-r|/2$, namely $0$, $1/2$, $1/2$, and $1$. The final cut $L=\{1,3\}$, $R=\{2,4\}$ has size $4$, while Theorem~\ref{thm:cut} guarantees only $m/2=2$. This is a largest possible cut, because no cut can have more than $m=4$ crossing edges; the path example in Section~\ref{ch13:sec:construction} shows that the construction does not always do this well.}
\sol{13.4}{Let $A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4$ be the bad events. The union bound (Proposition~\ref{ch13:prop:union}) gives
\[
\Prob(A_1\cup A_2\cup A_3\cup A_4)\le\sum_{j=1}^{4}\Prob(A_j)\le4\cdot0.2=0.8.
\]
So the complement of the union, the event that all four bad events are avoided, has probability at least $1-0.8=0.2>0$, and therefore contains an outcome of positive probability (Section~\ref{ch13:sec:model}). That outcome belongs to none of the four bad events.

With the bound $0.25$, the same calculation gives only $\Prob(A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_4)\le4\cdot0.25=1$, which is true of every event, so the argument proves nothing. In fact the conclusion can fail: in a uniform space with four outcomes $\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3,\omega_4$, the events $A_j=\{\omega_j\}$ each have probability $0.25$, but their union is the whole sample space, so no outcome avoids them all. It can also hold: in the same space, the events $A_1=A_2=A_3=A_4=\{\omega_1\}$ each have probability $0.25$, and $\omega_2$ avoids them all. So the information given does not decide the question.}
\sol{13.5}{Toss one fair coin, and let $X$ be $0$ if it shows $0$ and $10$ if it shows $1$. Then $\E X=\tfrac12\cdot0+\tfrac12\cdot10=5$, but $X$ takes the value $0$ at an outcome of probability $1/2$.

What the expectation does guarantee is Proposition~\ref{ch13:prop:above-average}: in a finite probability space, some outcome $\omega$ of positive probability has $X(\omega)\ge\E X$, and some outcome $\omega$ of positive probability has $X(\omega)\le\E X$. Here these are the outcomes where the coin shows $1$ and $0$, respectively.}
\sol{13.6}{Markov's inequality (Proposition~\ref{ch13:prop:markov}) with $a=12$ gives
\[
\Prob(X\ge12)\le\frac{\E X}{12}=\frac3{12}=\frac14.
\]
For an example where equality holds, take a uniform space with four outcomes $\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3,\omega_4$, and let $X(\omega_1)=12$ and $X(\omega_2)=X(\omega_3)=X(\omega_4)=0$. Then $X$ is nonnegative, $\E X=12/4=3$, and $\Prob(X\ge12)=\Prob(\{\omega_1\})=1/4$, exactly the bound. So Markov's inequality cannot be improved in general.

To find such an example, ask when both estimates in the proof of Markov's inequality are equalities: at outcomes of positive probability, the first needs $X=a$ wherever $X\ge a$, and the second needs $X=0$ wherever $X<a$. So $X$ should take only the values $0$ and $12$ there, and then $\E X=12\,\Prob(X=12)=3$ forces $\Prob(X=12)=1/4$.}
