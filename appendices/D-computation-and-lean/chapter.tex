\chapter{Running the examples on a computer}\label{app:practical}
None of the proofs in this book needs a computer. A computer helps in two other ways: it can carry out a finite experiment exactly, and it can check a formal proof. This appendix shows how to do both for the examples in the book. Sections~\ref{appD:sec:python} and~\ref{appD:sec:fractions} explain the small part of the programming language Python that the experiments use, including exact arithmetic with fractions. Section~\ref{appD:sec:experiments} then gives three complete programs: the count of sign sequences from Chapter~\ref{ch:signs}, the assignment search of Chapter~\ref{ch:matching}, and the biased iteration of Chapter~\ref{ch:capstone}. Each program runs exactly as printed, with nothing beyond a standard installation of Python. The last section explains how to check the Lean examples of Chapters~\ref{ch:lean1} and~\ref{ch:lean2}.
