\section*{Before moving on}
Close the book and check that you can do the following in your own words. State the converse and the contrapositive of ``if an integer is divisible by four, then it is even,'' and say which of the three are true and why. Explain, using choices made in order, why statement (A) of Section~\ref{ch02:sec:quantifiers} is true and statement (B) is false; then write the negation of (A) and say what it claims. Explain why the negation of an ``and'' statement is an ``or'' statement, and why ``every integer that is both even and odd is larger than $100$'' is true but tells us nothing about any integer. Show that for every positive $\eps$ some positive integer $N$ satisfies $1/N<\eps$, and say why $N$ must be allowed to depend on $\eps$. Finally, find all real numbers $x$ with $x^2>4$ without losing the negative ones.

These skills return in the definition of a limit (Chapter~\ref{ch:limits}), whose meaning depends on the order of three quantifiers, in Hall's theorem (Chapters~\ref{ch:matching} and~\ref{ch:hall}), and in the proof assistant Lean (Chapters~\ref{ch:lean1} and~\ref{ch:lean2}), which usually proves an existence statement by supplying an actual witness.
