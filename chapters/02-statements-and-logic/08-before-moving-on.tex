\section*{Before moving on}
Close the book and check that you can do the following in your own words. Take the statement ``if an integer is divisible by four, then it is even,'' state its converse and its contrapositive, and say which of the three are true and why. Explain why statement (A) of Section~\ref{ch02:sec:quantifiers} is true and statement (B) is false, using the idea of choices made in order. Write the negation of (A) and say in words what it claims. Explain why the negation of an ``and'' statement is an ``or'' statement, and why ``every integer that is both even and odd is larger than $100$'' is true but tells us nothing about any integer. Show that, for every positive $\eps$, some positive integer $N$ satisfies $1/N<\eps$, and say why $N$ must be allowed to depend on $\eps$. Finally, find all real numbers $x$ with $x^2>4$ without losing the negative ones.

These skills come back throughout the book. In Chapter~\ref{ch:limits}, the definition of a limit is a statement with three quantifiers, and its meaning depends on their order. In Chapters~\ref{ch:matching} and~\ref{ch:hall}, the gap between ``each student has an acceptable task'' and ``all the students can be given different acceptable tasks'' leads to Hall's theorem. In Chapters~\ref{ch:lean1} and~\ref{ch:lean2}, the proof assistant Lean writes implications and quantifiers explicitly, and the usual way to prove an existence statement there is to supply an actual witness together with a proof that it has the required property.
