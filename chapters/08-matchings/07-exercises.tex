\section*{Exercises}
Exercises~\ref{ex:8.1}--\ref{ex:8.4} are core exercises, and Exercises~\ref{ex:8.5} and~\ref{ex:8.6} are transfer exercises. Complete solutions are in Appendix~\ref{app:solb}.
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:8.1}In the second Ada--Ben example of Section~\ref{ch08:sec:model}, Ada accepts tasks $1$ and $2$, and Ben accepts only task~$1$. Give a maximal matching that is not maximum, and prove both assertions.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:8.2}Students $a$, $b$, $c$ accept the tasks $N(a)=\{1,2\}$, $N(b)=\{1,3\}$, $N(c)=\{2\}$. Start from the matching $\{a\text{--}2,\ b\text{--}1\}$. Use the search of Section~\ref{ch08:sec:search} to find an augmenting path, and write down the matching obtained by flipping along it.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:8.3}Three students each accept exactly the tasks $1$ and $2$. Give a shortage certificate, and explain why the fact that every student has two acceptable tasks is not enough for a full assignment.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:8.4}The proof of Lemma~\ref{thm:augment} uses the fact that an augmenting path has no repeated vertex. (a)~Explain where the proof uses this fact. Give an example of a matching and an alternating walk from an unmatched student to an unused task that visits some vertex twice, such that flipping along the walk does not give a matching. (b)~Suppose that some alternating walk leads from an unmatched student to an unused task. Prove that a shortest such walk has no repeated vertex, so that it is an augmenting path.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:8.5}A procedure increases the size of a matching by one at every step. Explain why this fact alone does not prove that the procedure stops, and why the finiteness of the set $L$ of students supplies the missing ingredient. State an upper bound on the number of augmentations.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:8.6}Suppose that the complete search of Section~\ref{ch08:sec:search}, started at an unmatched student $x$, fails, and let $A$ and $B$ be the sets of students and tasks it reached. Prove carefully that $N(A)=B$, treating matched and unmatched edges separately, and point out exactly where the completeness of the search is used.\end{exercise}
