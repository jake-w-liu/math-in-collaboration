\section{An exact finite experiment}
There are $2^n$ sign sequences of length $n$, because each of the $n$ positions independently holds one of two signs. For small $n$ a computer can examine all of them. The table below records the result of such an exhaustive search for $1\le n\le13$, computed with exact integer arithmetic; Appendix~\ref{app:practical} contains a short program that reproduces it. The search counts Barker sequences, and sequences whose periodic correlations $C_s$ vanish at every nonzero shift (equivalently, by the previous sections, first rows of circulant sign Hadamard matrices). Every sequence is counted separately, so a sequence, its negative, its reversal, and its rotations each count once whenever they are different lists.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}r rr@{}}\toprule
Length $n$ & Barker sequences & Sequences with $C_s=0$ for $1\le s<n$\\\midrule
1&2&2\\2&4&0\\3&4&0\\4&8&8\\5&4&0\\6&0&0\\7&4&0\\8&0&0\\9&0&0\\10&0&0\\11&4&0\\12&0&0\\13&4&0\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The table is evidence about exactly this finite range of lengths. It cannot rule out a sequence at a larger, untested length. The number of cases doubles each time the length increases by one, so a search up to length $60$ would already involve more than $10^{18}$ sequences. Simply testing more cases is therefore not a mathematical strategy on its own; a proof is needed to say anything about all lengths.

The table is consistent with what we have proved. Every nonzero entry in the last column occurs at length $1$ or $4$, in agreement with Proposition~\ref{thm:circulantnecessary}, which allows only $1,4,16,36,\ldots$ in this range. All counts are even, as they must be: negating every sign preserves all correlations (Chapter~6), and a nonempty sequence is never equal to its own negative, so the qualifying sequences come in pairs. One could halve the search by fixing the first sign to be $1$; the counts in the table were computed without this reduction, and the evenness of every entry is a small check on the computation.
