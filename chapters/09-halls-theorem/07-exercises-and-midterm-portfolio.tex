\section*{Exercises and midterm portfolio}
Exercises~\ref{ex:9.1}--\ref{ex:9.4} are core exercises, and Exercise~\ref{ex:9.5} is a transfer exercise. Exercise~\ref{ex:9.6} is the midterm portfolio; it draws on both this chapter and Chapter~\ref{ch:matching}. Complete solutions are in Appendix~\ref{app:solb}, where the solution to Exercise~\ref{ex:9.6} is a model portfolio.
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:9.1}Give a graph with four students and four tasks in which every student accepts at least two tasks and $|N(L)|=|L|$, but no matching saturates $L$. Find a group $S$ of students with $|N(S)|<|S|$, and explain why the tests for single students and for the whole class do not detect the problem.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:9.2}Let $N(a)=\{1\}$, $N(b)=\{1,2\}$ and $N(c)=\{2,3\}$. Check Hall's condition for every subset of $L=\{a,b,c\}$, find every nonempty proper tight subset, and find a matching that saturates $L$.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:9.3}In Case~1 of the inductive proof in Section~\ref{ch09:sec:inductive}, prove that $|N(S)\cup N(T)|=|N(S)|+|N(T)\setminus N(S)|$. Do not assume that $N(S)$ and $N(T)$ are disjoint; in the example of that section they are not.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:9.4}In Case~2 of the inductive proof, a nonempty proper subset $S$ of $L$ satisfies $|N(S)|>|S|$. Explain why it follows that $|N(S)|\ge|S|+1$. Would the same step be valid if $|N(S)|$ and $|S|$ were replaced by arbitrary real numbers?\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:9.5}Let $d$ be a positive integer. Suppose that every student accepts exactly $d$ tasks and that every task is acceptable to at most $d$ students. Use double counting (Chapter~\ref{ch:graphs}) and Hall's theorem to prove that a full assignment exists. Where does your proof use $d>0$?\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:9.6}\textbf{Midterm portfolio.} Assemble a short portfolio with four parts.
(a)~For the graph of Exercise~\ref{ex:9.2}, give a matching that saturates $L$, and explain how a reader can check it.
(b)~For a graph with no such matching, such as the one you found in Exercise~\ref{ex:9.1}, give a group $S$ of students with $|N(S)|<|S|$, explain how a reader can check it, and explain why it proves that no full assignment exists.
(c)~Write out, in your own words, a complete proof that Hall's condition implies the existence of a matching that saturates $L$. You may follow either proof in this chapter.
(d)~Write a paragraph that explains the difference between a maximal and a maximum matching, and why a greedy assignment can get stuck even when a full assignment exists.\end{exercise}
