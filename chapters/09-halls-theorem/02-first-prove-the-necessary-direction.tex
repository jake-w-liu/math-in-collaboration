\section{First prove the necessary direction}\label{ch09:sec:necessary}
We begin with the easier half of Theorem~\ref{thm:hall}: if a full assignment exists, then every group passes its test. This is the argument that Section~\ref{ch08:sec:shortage} used for shortage sets, written as a direct proof.

\begin{proof}[Proof that Hall's condition is necessary]
Suppose that a matching $M$ saturates $L$, and let $S$ be any subset of $L$. Each student $s\in S$ lies on exactly one edge of $M$: on at least one because $M$ saturates $L$, and on at most one because no two edges of $M$ share an endpoint. Call the task at the other end of that edge $f(s)$. Since $s$--$f(s)$ is an edge, $s$ accepts $f(s)$, so $f(s)\in N(S)$. This defines a function $f:S\to N(S)$.

The function $f$ is injective: if two different students $s\ne s'$ had $f(s)=f(s')$, that task would be an endpoint of two different edges of $M$, which a matching does not allow. By Section~\ref{ch03:sec:injsurj}, an injective function from a finite set $S$ to a finite set $N(S)$ produces $|S|$ different members of $N(S)$, so $|S|\le|N(S)|$. (When $S=\varnothing$, $f$ has no inputs and this reads $0\le0$.) Since $S$ was an arbitrary subset of $L$, condition~\eqref{eq:hall} holds.
\end{proof}

For example, in the graph of Section~\ref{sec:matching-trace}, with
\[
N(a)=\{1,2\},\qquad N(b)=\{1\},\qquad N(c)=\{2,3\}
\]
and the matching $b$--$1$, $a$--$2$, $c$--$3$, the group $S=\{a,b\}$ gives $f(a)=2$ and $f(b)=1$: two different tasks in $N(S)=\{1,2\}$, as Hall's condition requires.

The other direction is harder. The inequalities in \eqref{eq:hall} say nothing directly about which student should receive which task, yet from them alone we must produce an assignment in which the choices for all the students fit together at once.
