\section{The finished investigation as a short paper}\label{ch18:sec:paper}
An investigation is finished only when it has been written up so that another reader can check it without seeing your notes or your conversations with an assistant. For a project like this one, a short paper in four parts works well.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Introduction.} State the question, explain with the constant bias why the first guess is false, and say which questions replace it: how far from $x_*$ the points can be, and when they still converge.
\item \emph{Theory.} State the setting precisely, including the model of the update errors and the requirement that every $y_n$ lies in $X$. Then give Theorem~\ref{thm:inexact} with its proof, and the example showing that the factor $1/(1-q)$ cannot be lowered.
\item \emph{Experiments.} Present the exact computation of Section~\ref{ch18:sec:experiment}, with the program and enough detail that anyone can run it again and get the same output; such a computation is called \emph{reproducible}. Experiments illustrate the proved statements; they do not replace the proofs.
\item \emph{Conclusion.} Say what the results do not cover. The points must stay in $X$. The factor $q$ must be less than $1$: for $q=1$ the map need not have a fixed point, as $x\mapsto x+1$ on $\RR$ shows, and even when it has one, the sum $1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}$ behind part~2 equals $n$, so the bound $e_0+n\eta$ grows without limit when $\eta>0$. And bounded update errors that do not tend to zero may leave the points converging to a point other than $x_*$, or not converging at all (Section~\ref{ch18:sec:sharp}).
\end{itemize}

A paper should also say what is new in it. Here the honest statement is that the project reconstructs and analyzes a standard argument. It must not claim novelty merely because the student, or the assistant, had not met the result before: as \emph{To the reader} pointed out, a result you discover yourself may be correct and still be well known. Before claiming that a result is new in research, compare its exact statement and assumptions with the published literature, especially the \emph{primary sources}, the papers and books in which results first appeared. An unsuccessful search, in a library catalogue or by an assistant, shows only that this search did not find the result.
