\section{Can the constant be improved?}\label{ch18:sec:sharp}
Part~2 of Theorem~\ref{thm:inexact} says that, after many steps, the error is at most a little more than $\eta/(1-q)$. Is the factor $1/(1-q)$ really needed? Our proof used the triangle inequality, which may give away more than necessary, so perhaps a cleverer proof would give a smaller factor. To find out, we test the bound on the assistant's example from Section~\ref{ch18:sec:guess}.

Take $X=\RR$, $T(x)=qx$ and $y_0=0$, let $\eta\ge0$, and add exactly $\eta$ at every update:
\[
 y_{n+1}=qy_n+\eta\qquad\text{for every }n\in\NN.
\]
Section~\ref{ch18:sec:guess} checked that $T$ is a contraction with factor $q$, that its fixed point is $x_*=0$, and that every update error is $\eta$. The first few points are $y_1=\eta$, $y_2=q\eta+\eta$ and $y_3=q^2\eta+q\eta+\eta$, which suggests
\[
 y_n=\eta\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^k
     =\eta\,\frac{1-q^n}{1-q}\qquad\text{for every }n\in\NN.
\]
A pattern in the first few terms is only a guess, so we prove the formula by induction. At $n=0$ it gives $\eta(1-1)/(1-q)=0$, which is $y_0$. Now assume the formula for some $n$. Then
\begin{align*}
 y_{n+1}
 &=q\,\frac{\eta(1-q^n)}{1-q}+\eta\\
 &=\frac{\eta(q-q^{n+1})+\eta(1-q)}{1-q}\\
 &=\frac{\eta(1-q^{n+1})}{1-q},
\end{align*}
which is the formula for $n+1$. So it holds for every $n\in\NN$. Since $q^n\to0$, the points approach $\eta/(1-q)$, as the assistant claimed. There is also a second way to see where they go: the update $y\mapsto qy+\eta$ is itself a contraction with factor $q$, and its fixed point, the solution of $y=qy+\eta$, is $\eta/(1-q)$. So by Theorem~\ref{thm:banach} the points converge to $\eta/(1-q)$, the fixed point of the biased map, not to the fixed point $0$ of $T$.

Now compare the formula with the theorem. The starting error is $e_0=|0-0|=0$. Since $\eta\ge0$ and $0<q^n\le1$, every $y_n$ is nonnegative, so
\[
 e_n=|y_n-0|=y_n=\eta\,\frac{1-q^n}{1-q}.
\]
The bound in part~2 is $q^n\cdot0+\eta(1-q^n)/(1-q)$, which is exactly the same number. In this example the bound holds with equality at every step, so no better bound of any kind is possible here.

In particular, the factor $1/(1-q)$ cannot be replaced by a smaller number. Suppose someone proposes a number $K<1/(1-q)$ and claims that every iteration satisfying our assumptions, with all update errors at most $\eta$, obeys $e_n\le q^ne_0+K\eta$ for every $n\ge1$. Take $\eta>0$ in the example. There the claim says $\eta(1-q^n)/(1-q)\le K\eta$. Dividing by $\eta$ and multiplying by $1-q$, both positive, turns this into $1-q^n\le K(1-q)$, that is,
\[
 q^n\ge1-K(1-q).
\]
The right side is positive, because $K(1-q)<1$. Since $q^n\to0$, the inequality fails for every sufficiently large $n$. So the proposed bound is false for this example, and not just at one step but at every step from some point on.

An upper bound that holds for every example in some class is called \emph{sharp} if it cannot be lowered: every smaller proposed bound fails for some example in the class. We have just shown that the factor $1/(1-q)$ in part~2 is sharp for the class of all iterations that satisfy our assumptions.

A sharp bound on the distance to $x_*$ still does not tell us whether the points converge. Let $T(x)=0$ on $\RR$, let $\eta>0$, and take $y_n=(-1)^n\eta$, so that the points are $\eta,-\eta,\eta,-\eta,\ldots$. The map $T$ sends every point to $0$, so $|T(x)-T(y)|=0\le q|x-y|$ for every $q$ with $0<q<1$, and its fixed point is $0$. Every update error is
\[
 |y_{n+1}-T(y_n)|=|(-1)^{n+1}\eta-0|=\eta,
\]
and every error is $e_n=\eta$, so the distance to the fixed point never grows. Yet the points jump back and forth between $\eta$ and $-\eta$ and do not converge; Exercise~\ref{ex:18.3} asks you to check all of this carefully. Bounded update errors give a bound on the distance to $x_*$, but not convergence. Part~3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:inexact} needs its stronger hypothesis $\eps_n\to0$.
