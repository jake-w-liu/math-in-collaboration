\section{A numerical experiment with exact arithmetic}\label{ch18:sec:experiment}
To test the theorem on an example computed exactly, take $T(x)=(3x+1)/4$ on $\RR$, the map of Exercise~\ref{ex:12.1}. It is a contraction with factor $q=3/4$, because $|T(x)-T(y)|=\frac34|x-y|$, and its fixed point is $1$. Add a bias of $1/100$ at every update:
\[
 y_{n+1}=\frac{3y_n+1}{4}+\frac1{100},\qquad y_0=0.
\]
Every update error is $\eta=1/100$. Since $\frac14+\frac1{100}=\frac{13}{50}$, the recurrence reads
\[
 y_{n+1}=\frac34 y_n+\frac{13}{50}.
\]
This biased update has its own fixed point $p$: the equation $p=\frac34p+\frac{13}{50}$ gives $p=\frac{26}{25}$. As in Section~\ref{ch05:sec:recurrence}, we track the difference from it: put $z_n=y_n-\frac{26}{25}$. Subtracting the equation $\frac{26}{25}=\frac34\cdot\frac{26}{25}+\frac{13}{50}$ from the recurrence gives
\[
 z_{n+1}=\frac34 z_n,\qquad z_0=-\frac{26}{25},
\]
so $z_n=-\frac{26}{25}\left(\frac34\right)^n$ by induction (this is the formula of Exercise~\ref{ex:12.5}, with $a=3/4$ and $b=13/50$). Therefore
\[
 y_n=\frac{26}{25}\left(1-\left(\frac34\right)^n\right)\qquad\text{for every }n\in\NN.
\]
Such a formula, which gives $y_n$ directly from $n$ without computing the earlier points, is called a \emph{closed form}. Since $(3/4)^n\to0$, the points converge to $26/25$, not to the fixed point $1$ of $T$.

In Theorem~\ref{thm:inexact} we have $e_0=|0-1|=1$, $q=3/4$ and $\eta=1/100$, so $\eta/(1-q)=1/25$, and part~2 gives a guaranteed bound computed from known quantities, an \emph{error certificate} in the sense of Section~\ref{ch12:sec:theorem}:
\[
 \abs{y_n-1}\le
 \left(\frac34\right)^n+
 \frac1{25}\left(1-\left(\frac34\right)^n\right)\qquad\text{for every }n\in\NN.
\]
(Part~2 gives this for $n\ge1$; at $n=0$ it reads $1\le1$.) The closed form gives the true error exactly: $y_n-1=\frac1{25}-\frac{26}{25}\left(\frac34\right)^n$. The table compares the two, rounded to four decimal places. Some certificates therefore appear rounded down, such as $0.13610\ldots$ as $0.1361$; a certificate used as a guarantee must be rounded up instead (Section~\ref{ch12:sec:example}).

The true error first decreases, to about $0.0039$ at $n=11$. Between $n=11$ and $n=12$ the points pass the fixed point $1$, and from then on the error grows again, towards $1/25=0.04$. Someone who stopped at $n=11$ might conclude that the iteration converges to $1$; the closed form shows that the points overshoot and settle at $26/25$. The theorem adds something no table can: before any computing, it guarantees that the error never exceeds the certificate.

The last program in Section~\ref{appD:sec:experiments} of Appendix~\ref{app:practical} checks the closed form and the certificate in exact fractions for $n=0,\ldots,20$; changing \code{range(21)} to \code{range(31)} extends the run to $n=30$.

Other patterns of update errors give further experiments, again from $y_0=0$. With alternating signs, $y_{n+1}=\frac{3y_n+1}{4}+\frac{(-1)^n}{100}$, the computed points soon jump back and forth around $1$: those with even $n$ appear to approach $1-\frac1{175}$, those with odd $n$ to approach $1+\frac1{175}$. To see where $\frac1{175}$ comes from, suppose the even-indexed points settle at $1+u$ and the odd-indexed ones at $1+v$. Since $T(1+u)=1+\frac34u$, and an update from an even index adds $\frac1{100}$ while one from an odd index subtracts it, this gives $v=\frac34u+\frac1{100}$ and $u=\frac34v-\frac1{100}$. Substituting the first equation into the second gives $u=\frac9{16}u+\frac3{400}-\frac1{100}$, so $\frac7{16}u=-\frac1{400}$ and $u=-\frac1{175}$; then $v=-\frac3{700}+\frac1{100}=\frac1{175}$. This calculation assumes that the points settle; it does not prove that they do.

With update errors that tend to zero, such as $\frac1{100(n+1)}$ at step $n$, the points converge to $1$. All three patterns, constant, alternating and shrinking, have update errors at most $1/100$, so the certificate holds for each, but Theorem~\ref{thm:inexact} guarantees convergence to $1$ only for the shrinking errors. For the alternating errors it says nothing either way about convergence: a computation can suggest the answer, but only a separate argument can prove it.

\subsection*{Sharp, but not always attained}
Sharpness does not mean that the certificate equals the true error in every example; here it does so only at $n=0$. To see why, split the true error into its two sources:
\[
 y_n-1=-\left(\frac34\right)^n+\frac1{25}\left(1-\left(\frac34\right)^n\right).
\]
The first term, what remains of the starting error, is negative, because $y_0=0$ lies below $1$. The second, the accumulated bias, is positive for $n\ge1$, because the bias pushes upward. The certificate is exactly the sum of their sizes, because the triangle inequality ignores signs. For $n\ge1$ the two terms have opposite signs and partly cancel, so the true error is strictly smaller than the certificate. A bound that is larger than the true error in a particular example is called \emph{conservative} for that example.

So a bound can be sharp for a whole class and still be conservative for one of its members, and for a smaller class with extra structure a better bound may be possible. Which extra assumption rules out the examples that force the factor $1/(1-q)$, and what is the best factor under it? The alternating errors suggest a candidate: their points appear to settle only $\frac1{175}$ away from $1$, not $\frac1{25}$, because errors of opposite signs partly cancel.

Alternating signs alone are not enough, however; the assumption must also say something about the map. Take $T(x)=-qx$ on $\RR$, a contraction with factor $q$ and fixed point $0$ that sends every nonzero point to the other side of $0$. Let $\eta>0$, $y_0=0$ and $y_{n+1}=-qy_n+(-1)^n\eta$, so that the update errors are $\eta$ with alternating signs. Put $w_n=(-1)^ny_n$ and multiply the recurrence by $(-1)^{n+1}$; since $(-1)^{n+1}(-q)y_n=qw_n$ and $(-1)^{n+1}(-1)^n=-1$, this gives
\[
 w_{n+1}=qw_n-\eta,\qquad w_0=0.
\]
This is the constant-bias iteration of Section~\ref{ch18:sec:sharp} with $-\eta$ in place of $\eta$, so $w_n=-\eta(1-q^n)/(1-q)$. Hence $e_n=|y_n|=|w_n|=\eta(1-q^n)/(1-q)$, and part~2 holds with equality at every step. From the second update on, each update error has the same sign as $T(y_n)$, so it pushes the point further from $0$, and nothing cancels.
