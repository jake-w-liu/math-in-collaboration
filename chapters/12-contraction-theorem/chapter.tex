\chapter{The contraction theorem: an unfamiliar proof made readable}\label{ch:contraction}
\goal{Prove the contraction theorem: on a nonempty complete metric space, a map that shrinks all distances by a fixed factor has exactly one fixed point, and repeating the map from any starting point approaches it. Treat existence, uniqueness, convergence and the error bounds as four separate claims, each with its own short argument built from the tools of Chapter~\ref{ch:limits}. Use the error bounds to guarantee the accuracy of an approximation without knowing the exact answer, and see, by removing the hypotheses one at a time, why each of them is needed.}
