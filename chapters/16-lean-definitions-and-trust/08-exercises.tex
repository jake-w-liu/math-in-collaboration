\section*{Exercises}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:16.1}In the proof of \code{surj\_comp} (Section~\ref{ch16:sec:surj}), explain what \code{hb} and \code{ha} state. Explain also why the witness that the proof finally proposes must be an element of the domain $A$ of $f$.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:16.2}Define a predicate \code{join s t} that describes the union of the sets described by \code{s} and \code{t}, in the way that \code{meet s t} describes their intersection. Then state and prove in Lean the corresponding fact about preimages: for each \code{x}, \code{x} belongs to the preimage of the union exactly when it belongs to the union of the preimages.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:16.3}Suppose \code{h : Second r v w}. Write a Lean proof that $w\ne v$, and a Lean proof that some vertex $u$ gives a route $v\to u\to w$.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:16.4}An assistant cannot prove a theorem, so it adds the hypothesis \code{h : False} and then proves the result. Explain precisely what has changed, and why the fact that Lean accepts the new file does not answer the original question.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:16.5}Design a review in three steps that detects, in turn, a changed domain, a missing exclusion (such as the condition that a second out-neighbor is not a first out-neighbor), and an unfinished proof containing \code{sorry}. For each step, say what you examine: which part of the Lean file, or which output of Lean.\end{exercise}
\begin{exercise}\label{ex:16.6}Explain the difference between three kinds of check: running a finite test in Python, proving a statement about specific objects in Lean, and proving a universal theorem in Lean. Give an example of each, and say which cases your example covers.\end{exercise}
