\section{Conjunction means two obligations}\label{ch15:sec:conjunction}
To prove a conjunction $P\land Q$ you must prove both $P$ and $Q$, and from a hypothesis $P\land Q$ you may take either half (Section~\ref{ch02:sec:connectives}). The next theorem does both: it proves $Q\land P$ from $P\land Q$ by taking the two halves apart and putting them back together in the opposite order. On paper: if $P$ and $Q$ both hold, then $Q$ holds and $P$ holds, so $Q\land P$.
\par\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem and_swap (P Q : Prop) : And P Q -> And Q P := by
  intro h
  exact And.intro h.right h.left
\end{lstlisting}
\end{minipage}\par

Lean's name for $P\land Q$ is \code{And P Q}; the infoview displays it as $P\land Q$, and in the editor \code{\textbackslash and} followed by a space produces $\land$. After \code{intro h}, the fact \code{h : And P Q} is available and the goal is \code{And Q P}. A proof of a conjunction is a pair of proofs, one for each half: \code{h.left}, the first component, is a proof of $P$, and \code{h.right} is a proof of $Q$. In the other direction, \code{And.intro} builds a pair: if \code{a} is a proof of $R$ and \code{b} is a proof of $S$, then \code{And.intro a b} is a proof of \code{And R S}. The order of the inputs must match the conjunction being built: \code{And Q P} needs a proof of $Q$ first, hence \code{And.intro h.right h.left}.

A second version makes the two obligations visible as two separate goals:
\par\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem and_swap_two_goals (P Q : Prop) :
    And P Q -> And Q P := by
  intro h
  constructor
  exact h.right
  exact h.left
\end{lstlisting}
\end{minipage}\par
The tactic \code{constructor} applies \code{And.intro} without supplying its inputs, and each missing input becomes a goal of its own: \code{And Q P} is replaced by the goals $Q$ and $P$, which the infoview labels \code{case left} and \code{case right}. Each \code{exact} closes the first goal still open. Without the last line, Lean reports the error ``unsolved goals'' at the word \code{by}, where the proof begins, and displays the open goal $P$.

This is the formal counterpart of a paper proof that must establish two set containments (Section~\ref{ch03:sec:sets}), or both directions of an ``if and only if'' (Section~\ref{ch02:sec:implication}). After a long first half it is easy to feel finished. Lean tracks every open goal; on paper we must keep that list ourselves.
