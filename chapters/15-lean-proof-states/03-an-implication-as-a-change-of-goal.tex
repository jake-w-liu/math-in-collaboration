\section{An implication as a change of goal}\label{ch15:sec:implication}
Our first proof with more than one step proves an implication. Suppose $P$, $Q$ and $R$ are propositions such that $P\Rightarrow Q$ and $Q\Rightarrow R$; we want to prove $P\Rightarrow R$. On paper: assume $P$; then $Q$ holds, because $P\Rightarrow Q$; then $R$ holds, because $Q\Rightarrow R$. In Lean:
\par\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem implication_chain (P Q R : Prop)
    (hpq : P -> Q) (hqr : Q -> R) : P -> R := by
  intro hp
  have hq : Q := hpq hp
  exact hqr hq
\end{lstlisting}
\end{minipage}\par

Read the statement first. Several names before one colon, as in \code{(P Q R : Prop)}, are declared at once, so the parameters are three propositions and two hypotheses, \code{hpq} and \code{hqr}, and the claim is \code{P -> R}. Like the results of Section~\ref{ch04:sec:promises}, the theorem is a promise: supply three propositions and proofs of the two implications, and it delivers a proof of \code{P -> R}.

The statement that remains to be proved is called the \emph{goal}, the ``still to show'' entry of Section~\ref{ch04:sec:directions}. At the start, it is the claim \code{P -> R}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{intro hp} does what ``assume $P$'' does on paper: it adds a proof \code{hp} of $P$ to the available facts and leaves the goal \code{R}.
\item \code{have hq : Q := hpq hp} records an intermediate fact: its name, its type, and, after \code{:=}, its proof, the rule \code{hpq} applied to \code{hp}.
\item \code{exact hqr hq} says that \code{hqr hq}, the rule $Q\Rightarrow R$ applied to \code{hq}, proves exactly the current goal \code{R}. Lean accepts it, and no goal remains.
\end{itemize}
The table records the proof state after each step.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{1.3in}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}\toprule
Step & Facts added & Goal afterwards\\\midrule
Start & \code{P Q R : Prop}, \code{hpq : P -> Q}, and \code{hqr : Q -> R} & \code{P -> R}\\
\code{intro hp} & \code{hp : P} & \code{R}\\
\code{have hq} & \code{hq : Q} & \code{R}\\
\code{exact hqr hq} & none & none: the proof is complete\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The infoview shows each of these states in Lean's own notation. After \code{intro hp}, for example, it shows
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{}}
\toprule
\code{P Q R : Prop}\\
\code{hpq : P $\to$ Q}\\
\code{hqr : Q $\to$ R}\\
\code{hp : P}\\
$\vdash$\ \code{R}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each line above the symbol $\vdash$, called the \emph{turnstile}, lists something available: one or more names, a colon, and a type. The line that begins with $\vdash$ shows the goal. This is the proof state of Section~\ref{ch04:sec:directions}, with the ``known'' list above the ``still to show'' list. Lean prints \code{->} as $\to$; in the editor you may type either, and \code{\textbackslash to} followed by a space produces $\to$. If you have Lean installed, place the cursor at the end of each line of the proof in turn and compare the infoview with the matching row of the table.
